Research Observatory

Questions across the cosmic frontier.

HARK COSMOS LABS explores scientific questions from stellar evolution and planetary systems to black holes, cosmology, technosignatures and the mathematical foundations used to describe physical reality.

01 / COSMOLOGY

Origins of the Universe

Cosmic expansion, early-Universe physics, large-scale structure and the evolution of the cosmos.

02 / GRAVITY

Black Holes

Event horizons, relativistic gravity, accretion physics and the extreme environments where our understanding of spacetime is tested.

03 / STELLAR

Stars, Nebulae, Gas & Dust

Stellar birth, evolution and death; the interstellar medium; and the physical processes shaping luminous structures across galaxies.

04 / EXTRAGALACTIC

Galaxies Beyond the Milky Way

Galaxy formation, evolution, interactions, active galactic nuclei and structure on extragalactic scales.

05 / PLANETARY

Exoplanets & Planetary Systems

Planet formation, atmospheres, orbital systems, habitability and the extraordinary diversity of worlds beyond our Solar System.

06 / RADIATIVE

Multi-Wavelength Astronomy

Understanding the Universe across radio, infrared, optical, ultraviolet, X-ray and gamma-ray regimes.

07 / ASTROBIOLOGY

Life & Technosignatures

Habitability, biosignatures, the emergence of intelligence and possible observational signatures of distant technology.

08 / FOUNDATIONS

Multiverse & Quantum Cosmology

Theoretical models concerning the earliest Universe, quantum cosmology and possible cosmic architectures beyond the observable Universe.

09 / MATHEMATICS

Mathematical Foundations

Mathematical structures, models and abstractions that help describe physical systems from local dynamics to cosmic scales.

Scientific principle: HARK COSMOS LABS distinguishes established observation from active hypothesis and speculative theory. Subjects including extraterrestrial technological civilizations and multiverse models are treated as research questions, not established discoveries.

Civilization Horizon

From one planet toward the stars.

The Kardashev scale provides a conceptual framework for thinking about civilizations in terms of the energy available to them. Humanity remains below Type I. Type II and Type III civilizations remain hypothetical; none has been confirmed.

NOW · HUMANITY
Humanity

Pre-Type I

A technological civilization confined primarily to one planet, beginning to extend robotic and human capability beyond Earth while confronting planetary-scale challenges.

Type I

Planetary Civilization

A hypothetical civilization capable of operating at planetary scale, with increasingly integrated energy, information, scientific and spacefaring infrastructure.

Type II

Stellar Civilization

A hypothetical civilization operating on the scale of its star's available energy, potentially involving extensive infrastructure throughout a planetary system.

Type III

Galactic Civilization

A hypothetical civilization whose technological activity extends across galactic scales — raising profound questions about interstellar expansion, distributed intelligence and detectable technosignatures.

Type II / Stellar Horizons

When a civilization begins to think at the scale of a star.

What might intelligence become when the energy and infrastructure of an entire stellar system become accessible?

Hypothetical · No confirmed Type II civilization observed
Possible research directions include technosignatures, infrared excess, large-scale stellar-energy collection, autonomous infrastructure, interplanetary industry and long-distance communication. Dyson-swarm-like systems are hypothetical engineering concepts, not observed extraterrestrial structures.
Type III / Galactic Questions

What if intelligence became a galactic phenomenon?

If technological intelligence could expand across a galaxy, what observational signatures might it leave behind?

Hypothetical · No confirmed Type III civilization observed
Questions at this scale connect astrophysics, SETI, technosignature research, computation, interstellar communication, autonomous systems and the long-term dynamics of technological civilization.
